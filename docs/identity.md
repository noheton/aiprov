## Image brief

**Prompt (public).**

> Flat vector-style app icon: a round badge with a smooth gradient from bright teal (#00E5FF) at the top left to deep blue (#0072B8) at the bottom right. Inside the circle, in white negative-space silhouette only: a salmon leaping upward and to the right, body curved in an arc, tail down, head up, simple rounded shapes, a small dot for the eye. Behind it on the right, a waterfall drawn as three or four short white vertical strokes falling from the upper edge of the circle, with two small white splash dots at the bottom. Everything in one flat white colour, minimal, no text, no letters, no outline, no shading, no gradient on the fish, nothing outside the circle (transparent background), not photorealistic, not a copy of any existing character or logo. Same visual family as a white ram head, a white orangutan head and a white sea otter head in teal-to-blue circles.

**Variants.** Four images at 1024 × 1024; a version with the waterfall as two strokes and no splash dots; a version
with the fish filling two thirds of the circle. The icon must still read at 32 px, so the candidate with the fewest
strokes is preferred.

**Selection and record.** The owner chooses. The chosen picture goes to `assets/` with `assets/PROVENANCE.md`:
generator, model as displayed, date, sha256 of the prompt text as it stands in this file, how the file was obtained
(download or screenshot), processing steps, who chose it, marking, rights.

**Result (2026-10-25).** The owner chose the first result of the prompt above, made in the Gemini web app. The badge is
`assets/aiprov-icon.png` (512 px) and `assets/aiprov-icon-1024.png`; the README shows it. Record: `assets/PROVENANCE.md`.

## Rights of a generated picture

Read on 2026-10-25 (public pages, country version Germany):

- Google Terms of Service, effective 2026-07-30: "Some of our services allow you to generate original content. Google
  won't claim ownership over that content." and "Your content remains yours".
- Generative AI Prohibited Use Policy: the only relevant rule is not "misrepresenting the provenance of generated
  content by claiming it was created solely by a human, in order to deceive". `PROVENANCE.md` states the AI origin.

So the generator claims nothing and the repository licence (MIT) grants whatever rights exist in the picture. Whether
an AI-generated picture carries copyright at all is not settled in EU law; the licence therefore grants what there is,
and the picture is recorded as AI-generated, chosen by a human. Whether a release bundles it is the owner's decision
on this basis.
